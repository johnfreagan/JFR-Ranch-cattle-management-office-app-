WHY THE PRICE MATTERS MORE THAN USUAL RIGHT NOW. Whatever cost the opening count goes in at becomes the FIFO cost of that stock, and every treatment drawn off it carries that cost into the lot until the layer runs out. Getting it wrong does not correct itself next month; it works its way through the cattle. Redwing sets the price at the opening count by design — which is exactly why these two are worth settling before the count is posted.

Redwing and this program disagree about what a bottle of Enroflox cost. Both figures are internally consistent; they simply are not the same number.

What it could be
  • Freight and handling. Redwing's amount is what the invoice cost landed; ours is the drug line alone. On a 21-bottle order that gap is plausible.
  • A stale price here. Our figure is the last purchase we recorded, and Enroflox has moved.
  • A different pack or concentration bought at some point and booked to the same item.
How to settle it
  • Pull the most recent Enroflox invoice and read the per-bottle price and the freight line.
  • If freight explains it, Redwing is right and our catalog price should move up — landed cost is what FIFO is supposed to carry.
  • If it does not, the quantity or the item mapping in Redwing wants a look.

THE 250 mL LINE LOOKS LIKE FIVE BOTTLES OF MONEY AGAINST ONE BOTTLE OF PRODUCT. $2,596.71 ÷ 5 = $519.34 a bottle — within 0.3% of this program's $517.78, and within 3% of what Redwing's own 100 mL line implies. One bottle at $2,596.71 is not a price any supplier charges for 250 mL of Excede. The most likely reading is a receiving entry that took a case price against a quantity of one. John checked the shelf on 1 October and found one bottle, so the quantity is right and the money is not.

So all 2,650 mL of Excede on hand — 24 × 100 mL plus 1 × 250 mL — is being counted at Redwing's own 100 mL rate of $2.138917/mL, which is the rate corroborated twice over. That values Excede at $5,668.13 against the $7,730.11 Redwing carries.
