WHY THIS MATTERS. The ledger holds medicine in base units — mL, cc or doses — not in bottles. The Redwing report gives a container count and a dollar amount and never says how big the container is. Guessing the size would misprice every future dose of that drug by whatever factor the guess was wrong by, and it would do it silently. So these ten sit flagged until the size comes off the label. Write the size and circle the unit. The quantity column is a bonus — fill it where it is easy.

Container size means what ONE bottle, jug, cartridge or box holds — 500 mL, 1 gallon (3,785 cc), 100 doses, 50 count. Not how many you have.
If a product comes in two sizes, write both and say how many of each. Excede already does: Redwing carries it in 100 mL and 250 mL.
If the label disagrees with Redwing, the label wins. Note what it says and I will chase the difference.
